Abstract—Three-Dimensional Concrete Printing (3DCP) re-
quires reliable and non-destructive estimates of hardened concrete
strength. This study considers three related properties: compres-
sive strength ( Cs), splitting tensile strength ( Ts), and flexural
strength ( Fs). In the ConcreteXAI database of 4,420 specimens,
compressive strength is available for all samples, whereas tensile
and flexural measurements are missing for 21.7% and 60.2% of
samples, respectively. Consequently, only 39.8% of specimens con-
tain measurements for all three properties. Removing incomplete
samples reduces the usable data, while imputing missing values
can introduce errors into the learning process. Standard neural
networks may also produce physically implausible predictions,
such as tensile strength exceeding compressive strength ( Ts ≥C s).
This paper proposes Physics-Informed Masked Multi-Task
Learning (PIM-MTL), which learns shared representations
from all 4,420 specimens while masking the loss gradients for
unobserved targets. A differentiable quadratic penalty further
encourages predictions to remain within the specified strength-
ratio bounds: 0.06≤T s/Cs ≤0.18 and 0.08≤F s/Cs ≤0.25 .
On a held-out test set of 884 specimens, PIM-MTL obtains R2
values of 0.9818, 0.9857, and 0.9972 for compressive, splitting
tensile, and flexural strength, respectively. The corresponding
RMSE values are 1.3143, 0.1097, and 0.1262 MPa. The model
also records a 0.00% test-set violation rate for Ts ≥C s. These
results indicate that masked multi-task learning with physics-
based regularization can use incomplete experimental data while
supporting physically consistent strength prediction for 3DCP.
Index Terms—3D Concrete Printing, Mechanical Strength Pre-
diction, Multi-Task Learning, Physics-Informed Neural Networks,
Asymmetric Missingness, Masked Gradient Gating, ACI 318
Compliance.
I. INTRODUCTION
Automated construction and Three-Dimensional Concrete
Printing (3DCP) represent a paradigm shift in civil and
architectural engineering, offering unprecedented geometric
freedom, material optimization, and substantial reductions in
labor costs and construction timelines [1], [2]. Unlike traditional
mold-cast concrete, 3D-printed elements rely upon layer-by-
layer extrusion without temporary formwork. Consequently,
real-time assessment and predictive modeling of mechanical
strength are vital to verify structural integrity, buildability, and
long-term durability [3].
In structural concrete design (governed by standards such as
ACI 318 [4] and Eurocode 2 [5]), structural performance cannot

1) Compressive Strength ( Cs, MPa):Uniaxial crushing

2) Splitting Tensile Strength ( Ts, MPa):Diametral tensile

Despite their shared dependence on cement hydration ki-

for all three parameters is exceedingly difficult. Compressive
cylinder crushing is fast and standardized. Conversely, splitting
tensile and third-point flexural testing require specialized test
fixtures, heavy prism specimens ( 150×150×500 mm), and
extensive manual labor.
Consequently, real-world multivariate concrete databases
exhibit severeasymmetric missingness. In the international Con-
creteXAI benchmark database [9] comprising 4,420 multivariate
specimens, compressive strength is 100% complete, but splitting
tensile strength is missing in 21.7% of specimens ( N= 960 ),
and flexural strength is missing in 60.2% ( N= 2,660 ). As
shown in Fig. 1, only 1,760 out of 4,420 mixes (39.8%) possess
complete three-property labels.

Standard machine learning practices fail in this setting:
• Complete-Case Analysis (Row Dropping):Discarding
incomplete records discards 60.2% (2,660 mixes) of ex-
perimental data, destroying statistical power and inducing
severe laboratory selection bias.
• Artificial Imputation:Filling unobserved strengths with
mean, median, or k-nearest neighbor values introduces
synthetic artifacts that corrupt empirical gradients.
• Independent Single-Task Regressors:Training separate
models for each property ignores the continuum mechanics
coupling between compression, tension, and rupture.
• Unconstrained Neural Networks:Standard multi-task
networks frequently produce physically absurd outputs,
predicting negative strengths, tensile capacities exceeding
compressive strength (T s ≥C s), or out-of-code ratios.
To overcome these fundamental limitations, this work
develops aPhysics-Informed Masked Multi-Task Learning
(PIM-MTL)architecture. The primary contributions of this
paper are:
1) Masked Multi-Task Gradient Gating:A unified multi-
task loss formulation that enables training on all 4,420
mixes simultaneously. A binary observation mask ensures
that unobserved targets receive strictly zero backpropaga-
tion gradient, utilizing 100% of the data without synthetic
imputation.
2) Differentiable Continuum Physics Regularization:
Embedding ACI 318 and Eurocode 2 ratio bounds
(0.06≤T s/Cs ≤0.18 and 0.08≤F s/Cs ≤0.25 )
directly into the PyTorch optimization graph via a two-
sided quadratic hinge penalty evaluated on all specimens,
regularizing unobserved heads via microstructural me-
chanics.
3) Production-Grade Deployment Architecture:Incorpo-
rating a custom SafeBatchNorm1d layer that elimi-
nates runtime exceptions during single-specimen (N= 1 )
on-site inference, combined with Gaussian Error Linear
Units (GELU) and Cosine Annealing optimization.
4) Rigorous Quantitative and Physical Validation:
Demonstrating state-of-the-art predictive performance
(R2 >0.98 on all targets) and verifying a 0.00% rate of
physically impossible predictions ( Ts ≥C s) across an
isolated 884-sample test split.
II. RELATEDWORK
A. Machine Learning in Concrete and 3DCP
Recent literature has increasingly explored data-driven meth-
ods for concrete property prediction. Ali et al. [2] investigated
Support Vector Machines (SVM) and XGBoost regression
to predict the tensile and flexural strength of 3D-printed
concrete from mix formulations. Zhang et al. [3] examined
Random Forests and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) for
mechanical strength estimation, noting strong correlations with
binder composition. Similarly, Feng et al. [10] synthesized an
extensive international database to assess compressive capacity.
However, these prior studies treated strength prediction as
isolated single-target regression tasks on complete datasets,
discarding unobserved targets and neglecting multi-property
mechanics.
B. Multi-Task Learning and Incomplete Supervision
Multi-Task Learning (MTL) [11] aims to improve gener-
alization by sharing representations across related tasks. In
concrete mechanics, compressive, tensile, and flexural strengths
originate from the same microstructural hydration products
(calcium-silicate-hydrate gel, pore structure, and interfacial
transition zone). Hence, an encoder trained on multiple targets
should theoretically discover richer representations than disjoint
models. However, classical MTL architectures assume complete
target matrices. In sparse engineering datasets, naive masking
often leads to gradient imbalance or vanishing updates. Devel-
oping exact gradient-gating mechanisms is crucial to maintain
representation learning without distorting loss dynamics.
C. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN)
Physics-informed machine learning, formalized by Karni-
adakis et al. [12], integrates governing physical laws (such
as partial differential equations, conservation principles, and
empirical bounds) into deep neural loss functions. In structural
concrete research, Feng et al. [10] demonstrated that penalizing
unphysical strength behaviors improves out-of-distribution
robustness. In this paper, we extend physics regularization to
multi-task learning under extreme missingness: by evaluating
continuum ratio constraints onallsamples, the physical regu-
larizer provides meaningful gradient guidance to unobserved
prediction heads even in the total absence of ground-truth
laboratory measurements.

B. Continuum Mechanics and Civil Engineering Code En-
velopes
Concrete is a quasi-brittle composite material whose tensile
fracture is governed by microcrack propagation through the
Interfacial Transition Zone (ITZ). Over a century of empirical
testing and fracture mechanics formulations codified in ACI
318-19 [4] and Eurocode 2 [5] define strict physical relation-
ships between compressive, tensile, and flexural strengths.
1) Splitting Tensile to Compressive Ratio (R t):According
to ACI 318 Section 19.2.4 and Eurocode 2 Section 3.1.2, direct
and splitting tensile capacities scale with the square root of
compressive strength:

Accounting for aggregate mineralogy, Blaine fineness, and

A ratio below 0.06 signifies severe matrix delamination, while
a ratio exceeding 0.18 violates the Mohr-Coulomb failure
criterion for unreinforced cementitious paste. Moreover, sound
concrete can never be stronger in tension than in compression;
thus,T s < Cs is a mandatory physical invariant (R t <1.0).
2) Flexural to Compressive Ratio ( Rf ):Flexural strength
(Modulus of Rupture, Fs) evaluates extreme fiber bending

3) Hierarchy Between Flexural and Tensile Capacity:In
third-point bending, peak tensile stresses occur solely along the
extreme outer fiber, whereas splitting tension subjects the entire
diametral plane to tensile stress. Owing to Weibull’s weakest-
link volume effect and microplastic stress redistribution, appar-
ent flexural strength is consistently higher than splitting tensile
strength (F s > Ts). Unconstrained regressors that violate this
hierarchy produce structurally dangerous predictions.

VIII. DISCUSSION ANDENGINEERINGSIGNIFICANCE
A. Substantial Reduction in Testing Overheads
In 3D concrete printing and structural precasting, validating
third-point flexural strength (ASTM C78) requires massive
beam molds, extensive curing tanks, and destructive hydraulic
frames. By achieving an R2 of 0.9972 and MAE of 0.099 MPa,
PIM-MTL enables engineers to estimate flexural capacity
directly from rapid non-destructive tests (UPV , Electrical Re-
sistivity) and routine compressive cylinders, saving significant
material and labor costs.
B. Preventing Non-Conservative Shear Failures
In reinforced and printed concrete members without shear
reinforcement (ACI 318-19 Section 22.5), concrete diagonal
shear capacity ( Vc) is fundamentally governed by concrete
tensile strength ( Vc ∝
p
f ′c). Standard unconstrained neural
networks that overpredict tensile capacity risk non-conservative
shear designs, creating vulnerabilities to brittle catastrophic
collapse. By strictly enforcing Ts ≤0.18C s and eliminating
impossible predictions ( Ts ≥C s), PIM-MTL guarantees that
strength estimates conform to building code safety envelopes.
C. Robustness in Real-Time 3DCP Control
During continuous extrusion, printer nozzles adapt feed rate
and print speed based on early-age strength buildup. The
integration of SafeBatchNorm1d allows the network to
process single-mix sensor telemetry ( N= 1 ) without crashing,
enabling embedded on-line inference within real-time 3DCP
monitoring systems.
IX. CONCLUSION ANDFUTUREWORK
This paper introducedPhysics-Informed Masked Multi-
Task Learning (PIM-MTL), an end-to-end framework for
predicting the tri-axial mechanical strength of concrete under
extreme asymmetric missingness. By combining shared latent
trunk representations, exact masked gradient gating, and
differentiable ACI 318 / Eurocode 2 ratio bounds, PIM-MTL
eliminates the need for destructive row-dropping or synthetic
imputation while guaranteeing physical compliance. On an
isolated test split of 884 specimens, the model achieved
R2 = 0.9818 (Cs), R2 = 0.9857 (Ts), and R2 = 0.9972 (Fs),
while achieving a0.00% physically impossible violation rate.
Future research will extend PIM-MTL to incorporate early-
age rheological parameters (yield stress and plastic viscosity)
and interlayer adhesion bond strength under varying printing
time intervals.
