Abstract—3D Concrete Printing (3DCP) is an emerging ad-
ditive manufacturing technology that enables automated, layer-
by-layer construction of complex concrete structures, reducing
labour, material waste, and construction time. Despite its poten-
tial, 3DCP faces critical challenges related to process control and
long-term durability. This study focuses on developing a real-time
machine learning-based system to predict mechanical strength
(compressive, tensile, and flexural) from material composition
and process parameters. A suite of supervised learning algorithms
— Ridge Regression, Naive Bayes Regressor, XGBoost, Light-
GBM, and a PyTorch Artificial Neural Network — is trained and
evaluated using the ConcreteXAI dataset. The proposed pipeline
further includes a web-based interface that accepts CSV/Excel
input and returns strength predictions in real time. By integrating
experimental observations with predictive modelling and adaptive
control, the framework aims to enhance the reliability, structural
performance, and efficiency of 3DCP.
Index Terms—3D Concrete Printing, Additive Manufacturing,
Machine Learning, Mechanical Strength Prediction, XGBoost,
Artificial Neural Networks
I. INTRODUCTION
Concrete is the most widely used construction material in
the world, forming the backbone of modern infrastructure.
Despite its ubiquity, conventional construction methods remain
labour-intensive, reliant on formwork, and prone to human
error. In recent years, 3D Concrete Printing (3DCP) has
emerged as a paradigm-shifting technology that fabricates
structures layer by layer through controlled extrusion of a
cementitious material, eliminating formwork and enabling
complex geometries that are impractical through conventional
casting.
Research has demonstrated that 3DCP can potentially re-
duce overall construction costs by 50%–60% while improving
sustainability by optimising material use and reducing carbon
footprint. Realising these benefits, however, requires a thor-
ough understanding of the interplay between material science,
mechanical design, and process control.
During a visit to the 3DCP Laboratory at the National Insti-
tute of Technology Karnataka (NITK), Surathkal, we observed
the complete working mechanism of a large-scale 3D concrete
printer (Fig. 1). The visit provided insights into rheological
behaviour of the printable mix, bonding strength between
layers, and the influence of environmental conditions. One
of the major challenges observed is developing an ideal mix
design that simultaneously satisfies pumpability, extrudability,
and buildability — requiring fine-tuning of admixtures and
real-time process control.
In this context, the present study focuses on developing a
supervised machine learning pipeline to predict mechanical
strength from composition and process data, deployable as a
web application.
Fig. 1. 3D Concrete Printer at NITK Surathkal 3DCP Laboratory.
II. BACKGROUND
The conventional construction industry faces persistent chal-
lenges including low labour efficiency, high accident rates, a
diminishing skilled workforce, and rising costs from formwork
— a necessary but wasteful step in traditional concrete cast-
ing [1]. 3DCP has emerged as a solution, leveraging digital
fabrication to automate construction processes [2].
The foundational work was pioneered by Behrokh Khosh-
nevis at the University of Southern California through Contour
Crafting — a large-scale, on-site extrusion-based method for
rapid building construction [3]. A key innovation was the
integration of robotic trowels adjacent to the extrusion nozzle
to produce smooth, paint-ready surfaces while embedding
conduits for electrical and plumbing services.

III. LITERATUREREVIEW
The application of machine learning (ML) to 3DCP is a
burgeoning field. Predictive modelling has shown significant
promise in optimising mix designs and forecasting mechanical
performance, reducing the need for time-consuming experi-
mental trials [5].
Ali et al. [1] developed Support Vector Machine (SVM) and
XGBoost Regression models to predict flexural and tensile
strength of 3D-printed concrete from mix design parame-
ters (water-to-binder ratio, cement content, fibre properties),
demonstrating high predictive accuracy and establishing a
precedent for ML-driven mix design optimisation.
Building on this, Zhang et al. [2] employed random forests
and artificial neural networks to predict mechanical properties,
achieving high correlation coefficients. More recent work
has extended these approaches to predict interlayer bonding
strength using advanced ensemble methods such as stacking
models [4].
While these studies provide a robust framework for predict-
ing final mechanical strength from initial mix proportions, a
significant gap remains in real-time prediction deployable as a
user-facing tool. The existing literature has not fully addressed
end-to-end pipelines combining multi-model comparison, hy-
perparameter optimisation, and web-based deployment. This
study bridges that gap by providing an open prediction inter-
face for practitioners.
A. Problem Statement
To develop AI-based predictive models that analyse material
composition and process parameters to predict mechanical
strength (compressive, tensile, and flexural) of 3DCP struc-
tures, and to deploy these models as a web application that
accepts user-uploaded CSV/Excel data.
B. Objectives
1) Detailed study of parameters and features required for
predicting mechanical strength in 3DCP structures.
2) Data collection, exploratory data analysis, and design of
a preprocessing pipeline.
3) Training and comparison of multiple supervised learning
algorithms: Ridge Regression, Naive Bayes Regressor,
XGBoost, LightGBM, and ANN.
4) Evaluating model performance using R 2, MAE, and
RMSE, and selecting the best model per strength target.
5) Deploying the trained models as a web application that
accepts CSV/Excel files and returns predictions in real
time.

A. Dataset Collection and Description
The dataset utilised in this study is the publicly avail-
ableConcreteXAIdataset, encompassing evaluations of both
mechanical performances and non-destructive tests [6]. It
incorporates 18,480 continuous data points, derived from an
extensive 10-year laboratory investigation into concrete mate-
rials.
The dataset is structured across eleven columns. It includes
4,420 instances for tests such as Compressive strength (Cs),
Electrical resistivity (Er), and Ultrasonic pulse velocity (UPV).
Due to the complexity of fabricating specific prismatic and
cylindrical specimens, the dataset contains 3,460 values for
Tensile strength (Ts) and 1,760 values for Flexural strength
(Fs) [6].
The columns are categorised into categorical and numerical
variables. The categorical features include:
•Type of cement:Contains variations such as ‘CPO 30R
RS BRA’, ‘CPC 40R’, ‘CPC 30R RS’, and ‘CPC 30R’.
•Brand:Includes ‘CEMEX’ and ‘Apasco’.
•Additives:Features additions like ‘Op-
untia ficus indica’, ‘Starch fluidizer’,
‘Blast furnace slag 10%’, and ‘No additions’.
•Type of aggregates:Encompasses ‘Rounded’, ‘Crushed’,
‘V olcanic’, and ‘Recycled’ aggregates.

The input features encompass material composition and
process parameters:
•Material Composition:Type of cement, cement brand,
additives, type of aggregates, design compressive target

C. Predicted Parameters
The dependent variables predicted by the models are:
•Compressive strengthC s (MPa): Load-bearing capacity.
•Tensile strengthT s (MPa): Interlayer bonding quality.
•Flexural strengthF s (MPa): Resistance to bending and
interlayer failure.
D. Prediction Model Design
Preprocessing:Categorical features (cement type,
brand, additives, aggregate type) are encoded with One-
Hot Encoding; numerical features are standardised with
StandardScaler. AColumnTransformerwraps both
steps inside a unifiedsklearnPipeline.
Model Training:Five supervised learning algorithms are
trained independently for each of the three strength targets.
Allsklearn-based models are embedded inside the same
preprocessingPipelineso that feature encoding and scaling
are always fitted only on training data and applied consistently
to validation and test splits. Hyperparameter selection uses
5-fold cross-validation scored by R 2. The best estimator is
then refitted on the full 70% training partition before final
evaluation on the held-out 30% test set.
Evaluation Metrics:Every model is assessed with four
complementary metrics on the test split:
•RMSE(Root Mean Square Error) — penalises large
deviations heavily, reported in the same units as the target
(MPa).
•MAE(Mean Absolute Error) — robust average absolute
deviation, easier to interpret in engineering practice.
•R 2 (Coefficient of Determination) — proportion of vari-
ance explained; values closer to 1 indicate a better fit.
•AdjustedR 2 — penalises unnecessary predictors, com-
puted as1− (1−R2)(n−1)n−p−1 wherenis the sample size and
pis the number of features.
Web Deployment:The best-performing pipeline per target
is serialised withpickleand exposed through a web applica-
tion that accepts user-uploaded CSV/Excel files, preprocesses
the data, runs inference, and returns predicted strength values
— enabling practitioners to obtain predictions without any
programming knowledge.

In the context of 3DCP strength prediction, Ridge serves as

Limitations:Ridge assumes a linear relationship between

non-linear curvature without explicit feature engineering. It
therefore provides a performance lower bound against which
more expressive models are compared.

D. LightGBM — Light Gradient Boosting Machine
LightGBM improves upon standard GBDT through two
algorithmic innovations that are particularly relevant for the
ConcreteXAI dataset:
•Gradient-based One-Side Sampling (GOSS):Retains
instances with large gradients (high training error) and
randomly samples those with small gradients, reducing
computation while preserving the information-rich data
points.
•Exclusive Feature Bundling (EFB):After One-Hot
Encoding, the feature space includes many sparse binary
columns (one per categorical level). EFB bundles mutu-
ally exclusive sparse features into a single dense feature,
drastically reducing the effective dimensionality without
information loss.
LightGBM also usesleaf-wise(best-first) tree growth in-
stead of thelevel-wisestrategy of traditional GBDT. Given
a fixed number of leaves, leaf-wise growth typically achieves
lower loss but can overfit on small datasets — regularised here
vianum_leavesandmin_data_in_leaf.
Hyperparameter grid (5-fold CV):
•num_leaves∈ {31,63}— controls model complexity.

•subsample∈ {0.8,1.0}— row subsampling (bagging
fraction).

pling.

4) Why a Neural Network for Concrete Strength?:Concrete
strength is governed by highly non-linear physicochemical
processes — hydration kinetics, pozzolanic reactions, aggre-
gate interlocking — that interact in ways not captured by
linear or shallow tree models. The ANN’s layered architecture
allows it to learn hierarchical representations: early layers may
encode basic mixture ratios, intermediate layers may capture
interaction effects (e.g., cement–additive synergy), and the
final layer maps these to strength. The inclusion of BN and
dropout allows training on the moderately-sized ConcreteXAI
dataset without significant overfitting, as validated by the
smooth convergence of training and validation loss curves
(Fig. 8).

VII. CONCLUSION& FUTUREWORK
This study presents an AI-based intelligent framework for
predicting the mechanical strength of 3D-printed concrete
structures. A pipeline encompassing data preprocessing, multi-
model training (Ridge, Naive Bayes, XGBoost, LightGBM,
ANN), hyperparameter optimisation, and performance bench-
marking has been implemented on the ConcreteXAI dataset.
Gradient boosting and ANN models consistently achieve high
accuracy (R2 ≥0.88) across compressive, tensile, and flexural
strength targets.
The trained pipeline is being integrated into a web appli-
cation that accepts CSV/Excel uploads, enabling practitioner-
friendly real-time strength predictions without programming
expertise.
Future work will focus on:
1) Expanding the dataset with experimental records from
the NITK 3DCP Laboratory, specifically focusing on the
influence of supplementary materials like Nano-Silica
and Fly Ash on rheology and strength, for domain-
specific model fine-tuning.
2) Integrating real-time sensor data (extrusion pressure,
flow rate) into an adaptive control loop for dynamic
parameter adjustment.
3) Deploying the web application and conducting user
validation studies with civil engineers and construction
practitioners.
